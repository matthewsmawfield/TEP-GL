6.1 Limitations of the "Stack of Corrections"

Gravitational physics has historically advanced by refining the fundamental geometric framework rather than adding ad-hoc corrections. TEP represents a similar upgrade to the fundamental object, following the natural historical progression of physical theory:

  • Newton: Gravity is a Force; Time is Absolute.
  • Einstein: Gravity is Geometry; Time is Relative (Coordinate-Dependent).
  • TEP: Gravity is Geometry; Time is a Dynamical Field.

By treating the rate of proper time accumulation as a physical field with its own degrees of freedom (rather than a fixed function of the metric), TEP unifies the "stack" into a single framework. The "dark matter" anomaly is the observation that this field has spatial gradients.

6.2 CMB Lensing and the Integrated Sachs-Wolfe Constraint

A scalar field capable of generating year-scale reconstruction-space contrasts on cluster scales raises a critical question: what are the implications for the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB)? Two potential tensions must be addressed.

6.2.1 The ISW Effect

The Integrated Sachs-Wolfe (ISW) effect arises when CMB photons traverse time-evolving gravitational potentials. In TEP, the conformal factor \(A(\phi)\) contributes an additional term to the photon temperature perturbation:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:gl_isw} \frac{\Delta T}{T}\bigg|_{\text{TEP}} = \int \frac{\partial \ln A(\phi)}{\partial t}\, dt \end{equation}

For TEP to remain consistent with Planck constraints on the ISW amplitude, one of the following must hold:

  • Quasi-Static Regime: The scalar field evolves slowly (\(\partial_t A \ll H_0 A\)), so the TEP contribution to ISW is subdominant to the standard \(\dot{\Psi}\) term. This is natural if \(\phi\) tracks the matter distribution adiabatically.
  • Cancellation: In some scalar-tensor theories, the conformal ISW contribution partially cancels the metric ISW, leaving the net effect within observational bounds (cf. Amendola et al. 2008).

6.3 Interpretational Challenges

TEP effects mimic standard lensing signatures, making them difficult to distinguish without time-domain analysis.

  1. Static Degeneracy: Most lensing data are analyzed under static assumptions. In this limit, the conformal sector enters as a reconstruction degeneracy — the endpoint-determined clock-transfer map defined through $A(\phi) = \exp(\beta_A \phi/M_{\rm Pl})$ and the line-of-sight field profile $\phi(r)$ is absorbed into mass-sheet and delay normalization rather than into propagated arrival times (Section 3), while optical convergence and shear are carried by the scalar-backreaction and disformal channels.   2. Differential vs. Common-Mode Effects: The GW170817 constraint is often interpreted as excluding modified metric couplings. Conformal proper-time dilation is a common-mode effect for co-propagating signals, leaving the "speed of gravity" constraint satisfied while permitting significant time-domain phenomenology.

6.4 Reinterpreting the Evidence: Multipath vs. Single-Path

In the weak-field, perturbative-\(B\) limit the eikonal index is direction-dependent, \(n_{\rm eff}(\hat n,\mathbf x) \simeq 1 - 2\Phi/c^2 + \tfrac12 (B/A^2)(\hat n\cdot\nabla\phi)^2\): photons are retarded only when propagating along the scalar gradient. The Born deflection \(\alpha_i(\mathbf b) = \int \partial_i n_{\rm eff}\,dl\) and the reduced-deflection convergence \(\kappa_{\rm dis}(\theta) = -\tfrac12 (D_{ls}/D_s)\,D_l\,\nabla_b\cdot\alpha\) were computed on a Bullet-like two-subcluster geometry (0.72 Mpc separation, gas centroid lagging the bullet galaxies by 0.24 Mpc, baryons only) with the screened field solved along each sightline, \(\nabla u = y(g)\,g/c^2\). Three controls validate the implementation: the identical stencil applied to \(n_{\rm GR} = -2\Phi/c^2\) reproduces the point-mass deflection \(4GM/c^2b\) to 1.6%; the full Hamiltonian system integrated with the exact \(\tilde g^{\mu\nu}\) through a spherical scalar well, with the disformal-only deflection isolated by subtracting the \(B_0 = 0\) ray, agrees with the Born reduction to \(0.02\)–\(0.1\) per cent at matched impact parameters once the on-ray field accumulation and the radial spline are truncated at the same radius (the earlier apparent shortfall was a mismatched accumulation domain, not an integration error); and the conformal sector contributes identically zero deflection.

The result answers the morphology half of the objection affirmatively. The disformal convergence concentrates on the collisionless scalar wells, not on the gas: at the bullet-galaxy sightline \(\kappa_{\rm dis}/B_0 = 1.6\times10^{-7}\) versus \(6.8\times10^{-8}\) at the gas centroid, and the map peak sits on the main-cluster well to within a resolution cell. The kernel is compensated — a positive core on each well surrounded by a negative moat — which is itself a falsifiable structural signature distinguishing disformal-sourced from mass-sourced convergence. The "empty" lensing regions are not empty in the matter metric: they host the deepest \(\nabla u\).

The amplitude half remains adverse. Reproducing a peak convergence of \(\kappa \simeq 0.3\) at the galaxy positions requires \(B_0^{\rm req} \simeq 3.7\times10^{5}\) under the canonical halo convention — \(4.8\times10^{3}\) above the GW170817-transferred bound \(B_0 \lesssim 77.7\). Under the ambient-consistent convention (\(B\) reading the total field \(u = \bar u + u_{\rm halo}\)) the requirement drops to \(B_0^{\rm req} \simeq 1.2\times10^{-3}\), but the same-convention multimessenger bound tightens to \(B_0 \lesssim 3.1\times10^{-11}\) (Paper 19, step_63), worsening the exclusion to \(\sim 4\times10^{7}\). Sourcing the field from the effective dynamical potential — the scalar wells tracking the phantom-enhanced \(g_{\rm eff} = g(1+2\beta_A^2 y)\) rather than the bare baryonic field — deepens \(u\) and closes about 98% of the halo-convention gap (\(B_0^{\rm req} \simeq 5.3\times10^{3}\), excluded by a factor \(\sim 68\)), but does not close it. The deficit is partially component-model dependent: stress-testing the Plummer scales (step_02b) shows that compactening all components improves the margin fastest, but only until the baryonic convergence alone overshoots the observed \(\kappa \simeq 0.3\) at the galaxy sightline (\(a \lesssim 0.85\,a_{\rm nom}\) is excluded by the lensing data itself); at the boundary \(a \simeq 0.9\,a_{\rm nom}\) the requirement improves to \(B_0^{\rm req} \simeq 720\), a factor \(\sim 9\) over the bound. The residual gap therefore spans \(\sim 9\)–\(70\) across the defensible baryonic-model range, insensitive to the line-of-sight integration extent (doubling to \(\pm 8\) Mpc moves the margin by \(13\%\)). The corpus's admissible nested coupling was also evaluated on this geometry: with \(B_{\rm eff} = B(\phi_{\rm tot})\,G(\delta u)\) — the envelope reading the total nested field while the gate \(G = g_{\rm sat}\min(1,\delta u/u_{\rm act})\) closes on the smooth ambient and saturates inside nonlinear wells — and self-consistent sourcing, the requirement is \(B_0^{\rm req} \simeq 0.02\)–\(0.15\) across the gate bracket \(g_{\rm sat} = 10^{-3}\)–\(7\times10^{-3}\), against the same-convention host-well bound \(B_0 \lesssim 2.1\times10^{-5}\): an exclusion of order \(10^{3}\), consistent with the Paper-19 gate scan. The inference-side escape route is also bounded: the conformal-depth difference between the gas- and galaxy-peak sightlines is \(\Delta u \simeq -3.0\times10^{-6}\) (\(c\,\Delta u \simeq -0.9\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}}\)), corresponding to an X-ray emissivity and gas-mass inference bias of order \(10^{-6}\) — five orders of magnitude below what relocating the baryon dominance requires (and in any individual dissociative merger a \(\sim 1\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}}\) differential clock shift is masked by \(\mathcal{O}(10^{2}\text{--}10^{3})\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}}\) hydrodynamic shock and ram-pressure stripping velocities, making it a baryon-census consistency bound and a regime accessible only in large ensemble-stacked cluster gravitational-redshift surveys).

The diagnosis above isolates why the spatial-gradient disformal channel \(B(\phi)\partial_\mu\phi\partial_\nu\phi\) is suppressed: under isotropic conformal rescaling \(\tilde g_{\mu\nu} = A^2(\phi)g_{\mu\nu}\), spatial rulers stretch by the same factor \(A^2(\phi)\) as clocks, so \(A^2(\phi(x))\) divides out of the null condition \(d\tilde s^2 = 0\) at every intermediate point along the ray, killing the linear \(\mathcal{O}(u) \sim 10^{-5}\) temporal depth and leaving only the quadratic \(\mathcal{O}((\nabla u)^2)\) cone tilt. In TEP's cosmological ontology, however, space does not stretch: under the unstretched-space temporal metric \(\tilde g_{\mu\nu} = -A_{\rm loc}^2(u_{\rm grav}(\mathbf x))U_\mu U_\nu + A_{\rm sp}^2(u_{\rm drift}(t,\mathbf x))\Pi_{\mu\nu}\) (\(U_\mu \equiv -\nabla_\mu\bar\phi/\sqrt{2\bar X}\)), the \((3+1)\) ADM spatial geometry \(\gamma_{ij}=(1-2\Phi_N/c^2)\delta_{ij}\) remains unstretched while the spacetime lapse rescales as \(N_{\rm TEP}(x)=A(\phi(x))N_{\rm GR}(x)\) along the temporal flow. Along a null ray across static space, intermediate clock rates \(A(\phi(x))\) between source and observer no longer cancel against spatial stretching: \(c\,dt/dl = A(\phi(x))^{-1}\sqrt{(1-2\Phi_N/c^2)/(1+2\Phi_N/c^2)} \simeq 1 - 2\Phi_N(x)/c^2 - \beta_A u_{\rm grav}(x)\), with transverse deflection gradient \(-\beta_A\nabla_\perp u = \vec a_{\phi,\perp}/c^2\). Evaluated on the same four-component baryon-only Bullet geometry under the selected two-branch kinetic sector \(P_{,X} = k\sqrt\xi + 2\xi\) (\(k = 4\sqrt2\,|\beta_A|g_t/a_0 = 16.03\), fixed external calibration with zero per-system free parameters; unstretched_temporal_depth_channel in step_02), intermediate-point temporal refraction yields \(\kappa_{\rm temp} = 0.065\) (\(1\times\) temporal lapse) to \(0.131\) (\(2\times\) dynamical normalization) on top of \(\kappa_{\rm bar} = 0.241\) at the bullet-galaxy sightline, giving \(\kappa_{\rm tot} = 0.307\)–\(0.372\) against the observed target \(\kappa \simeq 0.30\). The bullet-half convergence peak lands at \(x = +0.352\ {\rm Mpc}\) (on the galaxies at \(+0.36\ {\rm Mpc}\), versus \(\kappa_{\rm tot} = 0.223\)–\(0.284\) at the shocked-gas centroid at \(+0.12\ {\rm Mpc}\)), \(\kappa_{\rm temp}\) is positive everywhere across the field (\(\min\kappa_{\rm temp} = +4.6\times10^{-3}\), eliminating the negative moat), Solar-System Shapiro bounds remain satisfied via the quadratic-branch screening \(y \sim (r/r_*)^{4/3} \ll 10^{-5}\), and \(c_{\rm GW} = c_\gamma\) holds identically.
